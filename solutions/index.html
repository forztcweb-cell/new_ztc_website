SYSTEM / 01 Enterprise Core

Core Agentic Intelligence

A Digital Operating Intelligence for Your Organization

Most businesses already have software. They have CRMs, spreadsheets, communication platforms, dashboards, project management tools, accounting systems, and dozens of other applications. Yet people still spend a significant part of their day moving information between those systems, following up with employees, monitoring tasks, making routine decisions, and ensuring that work actually gets completed.

• Understand business context: Use organizational knowledge, historical activity, operating procedures, and current objectives to inform decisions.
• Coordinate specialized agents: Assign different tasks to agents responsible for sales, finance, operations, customer support, research, and other functions.
• Execute real work: Update records, prepare documents, coordinate workflows, send approved communications, schedule follow-ups, and trigger actions through connected systems.
The Result Business owners and executives can spend less time supervising routine activity and more time building products, serving customers, developing strategy, and making consequential decisions.
SYSTEM / 02 Critical Care & Health

Healthcare Intelligence

Coordinating the Operations Behind Better Care

Healthcare is one of the clearest examples of an industry where operational inefficiency can have consequences far beyond wasted time.

• Hospital operations: Coordinate appointments, admissions, discharges, bed availability, departmental workflows, and administrative tasks.
• Doctor and staff coordination: Manage schedules, availability, task assignments, reminders, and operational handoffs.
• Patient communication: Support appointment confirmations, preparation instructions, follow-up reminders, and routine administrative queries.
The Result Less administrative friction. Better coordination between departments. More visibility into hospital operations. Fewer avoidable workflow delays. More time for healthcare professionals to focus on patients.
SYSTEM / 03 Logistics & Supply

Logistics and Fulfillment Intelligence

Making Complex Networks Work Together

A logistics operation is more than a collection of trucks, warehouses, packages, and delivery routes. It is a constantly changing network of inventory, orders, suppliers, transport capacity, delivery commitments, exceptions, and dependencies.

• Monitor order status, shipment progress, inventory levels, and fulfillment workflows.
• Coordinate warehouse activity, dispatch planning, and delivery assignments.
• Identify potential delays, stock shortages, route disruptions, and missed service commitments.
The Result Better operational visibility, fewer avoidable delays, more coordinated fulfillment, and a more responsive supply chain.
SYSTEM / 04 Physical Infrastructure

Physical Intelligence Infrastructure

Connecting Digital Decisions to the Physical World

Software alone cannot manage the physical world unless it can observe what is happening, interpret relevant signals, and coordinate an appropriate response.

• Integrate suitable sensors, cameras, connected devices, and industrial data sources.
• Monitor equipment status, asset utilization, environmental conditions, and operational anomalies.
• Detect patterns that may indicate equipment faults, unsafe conditions, or unusual activity.
The Result Improved asset visibility, faster incident awareness, more structured maintenance, and better coordination between physical infrastructure and the people responsible for it.

• Assist with transaction reconciliation and exception identification.

• Track budgets, expenses, cash-flow indicators, and outstanding obligations.
The Result Less manual financial administration, improved reporting discipline, greater visibility into financial performance, and more time for finance teams to focus on analysis and planning.

Reasoning and planning

Interpreting objectives, evaluating options against business rules, and organizing work into actionable, deterministic steps.
